Interceptor's First Spectrum Game
Crash described Message From Andromeda as Interceptor's first Spectrum game, after the company had made its name on the Commodore 64. It was coded by David M. Banner, with in-game graphics by Jean A. Banner and the inlay by Terry Greer, and cost £5.50. Sources: Crash review, Spectrum Computing.
Interceptor Micros
Interceptor was run by father and son Julian and Richard Jones from Tadley in Hampshire. It was set up after a partnership with Jeff Minter ended in 1982, and later added the budget labels Players and Players Premier and the premium label Pandora. See Wikipedia.
Instant Pictures
Crash noticed that the pictures appear almost at once because they are stored ready-made rather than drawn line by line, and that commands are typed in silence with no cursor. Crash also found that "SHOOT SOLDIER" fails and you have to type "SHOOT ALIEN SOLDIER", and that dead soldiers stay where they fell. See the Crash review.
Help From The Producer
The Crash reviewer admitted getting the solution to the control panel from Richard Paul Jones of Interceptor at the Earls Court Computer Fair. See the Crash review.

Rooms Start At Two
The room description table at Table_RoomDescriptions leaves rooms 0 and 1 empty, and the game starts in room 2, "In Deep Space,on patrol." (Messaging_InDeepSpaceOnPatrol), which is set at 63714. The current room is only ever written at 36395, and nothing passes it room 0 or 1. Forest At World's End also leaves rooms 0 and 1 empty, but starts in room 5.
